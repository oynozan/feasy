# Turn Feasy Into Mezo's Liquid Locker

Mezo won't give Feasy much cash. What it can give is permission, distribution and veMEZO, and the feature that would earn the most of all three is a liquid veBTC and veMEZO locker built on the ladder and booster Feasy already runs. The prize money is small. The current Buildathon pays **$7,000 in MEZO across two waves**, and the spring Encode hackathon paid **$30,000 in veMEZO**. No public source gives a grant size, a fund size, an application form or a list of past recipients for the Supernormal Foundation's programs, and none announces a Wave 3. The real doors are the Foundation's **Ignition Program**, which covers prototype to mainnet and fits Feasy today, and after it **Alpha Builder, Mezo Market featuring and the Founder Program**. The first two require a **third-party audit and a mainnet deployment**, and the Founder Program wants a production dApp with traction. Beyond them sits the BitcoinFi Accelerator, where Draper Associates and Boost VC invest for equity. Onchain incentives exist, but they are small in dollars. At MEZO's current price the chain's whole weekly emission is worth roughly **$18-38k**, so a gauge buys reach and APR, not runway. Mezo spends visibly on MUSD liquidity (2.25% of MEZO supply went to Aerodrome voters), on bringing BTC in, and on demand for MEZO, while it deals with a token about **90% below its April high**, thin MUSD pools and an October veBTC exploit. The five features, ranked: a liquid veBTC locker with a pooled veMEZO booster, fBTC as a pool pair and collateral, a wrapped-BTC intake from Ethereum and Base, no-loss prize savings on sMUSD, and an institutional share class through Mezo Prime. A realistic ask is tens to low hundreds of thousands of dollars in MEZO or veMEZO, paid against milestones Mezo already tracks: MUSD minted, BTC locked as veBTC and MEZO locked as veMEZO.

## Mezo's cash is small, and one audit gates the rest

Builder money on Mezo goes through the **Supernormal Foundation**, the non-profit steward Mezo launched on July 14, 2025. It offers "milestone-based" funding, co-marketing and technical help through the Alpha Builder program and future grant programs, and states no amounts ([Mezo blog](https://mezo.org/blog/p/7a444038-07c0-472b-954c-8019ce189e0e)). Its advisors are Arthur Hayes, Dan Held, Dovey Wan and Will Reeves of Fold ([Chainwire](https://chainwire.org/2025/07/14/mezo-launches-supernormal-foundation-to-accelerate-bitcoinfi-innovation-and-mezo-ecosystem-growth/)). The 2026 roadmap adds a continuing **Founder Program** for production dApps that integrate MUSD, along with more hackathons, community bounties, the **BitcoinFi Accelerator** and a planned **Grant Committee** to set treasury spending standards. The roadmap labels all of it "aspirational and subject to change" ([Mezo roadmap](https://mezo.org/blog/mezo-2026-roadmap)). The Buildathon post names the two follow-on programs that matter for Feasy: the **Mezo Ignition Program** for early projects moving from prototype to mainnet, and the **Founder Program** for working products with initial traction. Selected teams "may" get milestone grants, integration help and user reach ([Buildathon post](https://mezo.org/blog/mezo-buildathon-is-underway)).

The Buildathon itself is a small purse. Wave 1 (Oct 16-26) paid $2,000 in MEZO, and Wave 2 (Nov 2-15) pays $5,000. Final submissions are due **Nov 15**, and winners are announced with an online demo on **Nov 23**. There are two tracks, DeFi and Access & Distribution, and entries must integrate MUSD or MEZO ([Buildathon post](https://mezo.org/blog/mezo-buildathon-is-underway)). The page gives no judging rubric, no split of each wave's prize and **no Wave 3**. A third-party roundup claims a 20-team shortlist on Nov 16, live presentations on Nov 22 and KYB checks for winners. Those dates conflict with the official ones and could not be verified ([X roundup](https://x.com/_HEISEMMA/status/2117244375776248164)), but preparing KYB papers now costs little. In Hackathon 2, Mezo's judges scored demo quality, code, testnet deployment and protocol integration, and Mezo called the winners the start of a relationship with follow-on grants and bounties ([Hackathon 2 winners](https://mezo.org/blog/announcing-mezo-hackathon-2-winners/)). The first hackathon drew nearly 400 participants and paid 37,500 MUSD across nine winners ([Mezo roadmap](https://mezo.org/blog/mezo-2026-roadmap)). The separate Encode Club hackathon paid each track winner $5,000 in veMEZO plus a $5,000 veMEZO milestone grant from a $30,000 pool. It required both MUSD and MEZO and checked KYB before payout ([Mezo hackathon post](https://mezo.org/blog/the-mezo-hackathon-is-back/)). Its MEZO track asked for ways to make MEZO productive, which is what Feasy's booster already does.

Two rows matter right now. Mezo Academy shares **4M veMEZO across eight epochs** among wallets that lock MEZO or boost veBTC, with no application ([Mezo Academy](https://mezo.org/blog/mezo-academy/)), and that is exactly what Feasy's booster does. The sources don't say whether a contract counts or when the class's epochs run, so it's worth asking Mezo this week. The other is the audit. Alpha Builder asks for a live dApp with onchain activity, EVM compatibility, Mezo Passport integration and **an audit before mainnet** ([Mezo Alpha Builders](https://info.mezo.org/home/mezo-alpha-builders)). So the larger doors sit behind one audit. Aura's first funding request to Balancer, about $300k, was to pay for audits ([Aura blog](https://blog.aura.finance/more-about-aura-finance-and-our-launch-plans)). That is the precedent for making the audit itself the first milestone grant Feasy asks Ignition for.

The accelerator is the only route that has moved real dollars at scale. When the Foundation launched, 15 accelerator teams had raised **$2.25M combined**, led by Draper Associates and Boost VC ([Mezo blog](https://mezo.org/blog/p/7a444038-07c0-472b-954c-8019ce189e0e)), about $150k per team on average. Two cycles have deployed over **$3M** in total ([Mezo roadmap](https://mezo.org/blog/mezo-2026-roadmap)). That money is VC equity, not Foundation grants. Mezo's own backers include Pantera, which led a $21M round, plus Multicoin, Hack VC, ParaFi, Nascent, Draper Associates, Primitive and Asymmetric ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/287245/bitcoin-mezo-thesis-funding)). Ledger Cathay Fund led a later $7.5M strategic round ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/307778/bitcoin-scaling-network-mezo-new-funding)). Of these, only Draper Associates, Dan Held (Asymmetric) and Dovey Wan (Primitive) also sit in the accelerator or on the Foundation's advisory group, so they are the investor relationships that could lead back to Feasy. The research found no sign of Mezo or Thesis taking equity in outside apps except through the accelerator.

## Mezo's whole weekly emission is worth about $18-38k, so gauges buy reach, not runway

MEZO emissions started at **4,807,692 MEZO a week** on 2026-04-02 and fall to about 4.49M by April 2027 ([Emissions schedule](https://mezo.org/docs/users/mezo/emissions/)). Epochs last 7 days and start Thursday 00:00 UTC, and votes in one epoch set the next epoch's emissions ([How MEZO emissions work](https://mezo.org/blog/how-mezo-emissions-work/)). First, a rebase goes to veMEZO, and it shrinks as more MEZO gets locked. Then a Chain Splitter sends 20% of the rest to validators and 80% to the ecosystem. An Ecosystem Splitter sends 90% of the ecosystem share to **staking gauges**, about 72% of all rewards, and 10% to **non-staking gauges**, about 8% ([Gauges and splitters](https://mezo.org/blog/how-gauges-and-splitters-work-on-mezo/)). Staking gauges pay LPs and the MUSD savers who stake their sMUSD. That is how Feasy's sleeve earns MEZO. At the launch rate the staking branch gets about 3.46M MEZO a week before the rebase.

The price makes all of this small. Trackers disagree: CoinGecko shows **$0.0038**, CoinMarketCap **$0.0081** against an all-time high of **$0.1415 on 2026-04-13**, and DefiLlama $0.0074 ([CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/mezo), [CoinMarketCap](https://coinmarketcap.com/currencies/mezo/)). At any of those prices, the entire weekly emission is worth roughly **$18-38k**. For Feasy, MEZO emissions are good for APR headlines and for feeding its booster, not for paying a team.

| Mechanism | Who can use it | Permission needed | What it gives Feasy |
|---|---|---|---|
| sMUSD savings gauge | Anyone staking sMUSD | None | MEZO emissions. The staker's fee share goes to the gauge's veBTC voters, and Feasy wins it back by voting its own veBTC there |
| Pool gauges on Mezo Pools | Anyone, through `createPoolGauge` on PoolsVoter `0x4823…1f03` | Both pool assets must be whitelisted | A gauge for an fBTC pool, once fBTC is whitelisted |
| MEZO Gauges (non-staking, about 8%) | veMEZO voters, about 200k MEZO per epoch | Governance allowlist | Votes for MUSD pools on Curve, Uniswap and Aerodrome |
| Boost gauges (one per veBTC NFT) | Anyone posting any ERC-20 incentive | None | Rented veMEZO boost on Feasy's locks |
| veBTC voting income | veBTC holders | None | Swap fees and MUSD revenue from voted gauges, plus bridge and chain fees |

Anyone can create a pool gauge once both assets are on the PoolsVoter whitelist ([Deploying incentives](https://mezo.org/docs/developers/features/mezo-earn/deploying-incentives)). A new token like Feasy's share token is almost certainly not on that list, though, and the docs describe no whitelisting process. MEZO Gauges send about 200k MEZO per epoch to MUSD pools on Ethereum and Base, and only governance adds or removes pools ([MEZO Gauges docs](https://mezo.org/docs/users/mezo-earn/vote/mezo-gauges.md), [Introducing MEZO Gauges](https://mezo.org/blog/introducing-mezo-gauges)). Anyone can deposit any ERC-20 into a veBTC position's boost gauge to attract veMEZO votes ([Matching market](https://mezo.org/docs/users/mezo-earn/lock/vemezo/matching-market)). A gauge only pays when veBTC votes point at it, so winning emissions means owning veBTC or paying incentives. Mezo's docs describe no app revenue share, referral fee, builder fee or gas rebate ([MUSD fees](https://mezo.org/docs/users/borrow/fees.md)). That only means none was found, and one question to the team would settle it.

The most useful number is the gap between the two kinds of lock. DefiLlama shows **$64.56M of BTC in veBTC**, with a high of $69.88M on Oct 5 and a low of $33.62M on May 13. Against that sits only about **$1.68M of staked MEZO** ([DefiLlama Mezo Earn](https://defillama.com/protocol/mezo-earn)). So veMEZO is worth about 2.6% of veBTC. A position's boost, up to 5x, depends on its share of veBTC compared with its share of veMEZO ([Mezo Earn overview](https://mezo.org/docs/users/mezo-earn/overview)). If that holds, matching a veBTC share with an equal veMEZO share costs about a fortieth of the BTC's dollar value. That makes veMEZO the cheapest lever on Mezo. Testnet is the opposite: veMEZO is plentiful there, so Feasy's 100 veMEZO first lifted its two locks only to 1.04x and 1.03x. With the third lock in Nov 12's roll, the boosts read 1.02x to 1.81x. The exact boost formula isn't in the pages read, and both mainnet figures come from search snippets, so this needs a live onchain check before Feasy builds on it.

Mezo's docs contradict themselves in three places that matter. The gauges blog says veBTC voters can shift the splitter ratios by one point per epoch, while the governance page says the ratios are fixed ([Gauges and splitters](https://mezo.org/blog/how-gauges-and-splitters-work-on-mezo/), [Governance](https://mezo.org/docs/users/mezo/governance/)). The shortest veMEZO lock is one week on the veMEZO page but one year in a search summary ([veMEZO overview](https://mezo.org/docs/users/mezo-earn/lock/vemezo)). And the fees page sends interest and redemption fees to the stability pool at launch, while the savings vault page puts them into the 50/50 split with bootstrap-loan repayment ([MUSD fees](https://mezo.org/docs/users/borrow/fees.md), [MUSD Savings Vault](https://mezo.org/docs/users/mezo-earn/vaults/musd-savings-vault.md)).

When Mezo wants something, it spends whole percentages of supply. It paid **2.25% of MEZO supply** to Aerodrome voters over four weeks: 0.5% as liquid MEZO in week one, then 1%, 0.5% and 0.25% as max-locked veMEZO ([Aerodrome x MEZO](https://mezo.org/blog/aerodrome-x-mezo-2-25-of-token-supply-rewards)). A secondhand report says it set aside up to **2.5% of supply** for early depositors in its "Bring Bitcoin Home" pre-deposit vaults ([finanzen.net](https://www.finanzen.net/nachricht/aktien/mezo-opens-pre-deposit-vaults-to-migrate-bitcoin-liquidity-from-ethereum-back-to-bitcoin-native-infrastructure-15410512)). The token pools are there: 40% of genesis supply belongs to the community, including grants and dApp LP incentives, and the Foundation's 10%, meant for partnerships and ecosystem growth, unlocked at the token launch ([Introducing MEZO](https://mezo.org/blog/introducing-mezo)). At today's price, 1% of supply (10M MEZO) is worth only about **$40-80k**. A grant that sounds large in tokens is cheap for Mezo in dollars. What Mezo watches is dilution, and a permanent lock takes the granted tokens off the market for good.

## Mezo is paying for MUSD liquidity, MEZO demand and BTC that stays

Mezo's 2026 roadmap reads like a shopping list. Under **scaling MUSD**, it wants MUSD used across chains as collateral, a trading pair and something to spend, Mezo as "the home" of wrapped BTC (tBTC, WBTC, cbBTC, SolvBTC) earning yield, **more BTC yield vaults across risk profiles**, and more card partners beyond Bitrefill and Lolli. Under **MEZO utility and governance**, it lists simpler Earn onboarding, a voting UI, **a veNFT marketplace** for veBTC and veMEZO priced in BTC, MEZO or MUSD, **delegation** and the Grant Committee. It also wants institutional MUSD minting and veMEZO locking through Anchorage, an EVM upgrade, AI-agent payment rails and more ecosystem programs ([Mezo roadmap](https://mezo.org/blog/mezo-2026-roadmap)). The research found no dated numeric target from Matt Luongo or the team.

Mezo's problems explain those priorities. On liquidity, Mezo's own Gauges post says thin pools limit how much MUSD can be borrowed and put pressure on the peg ([Introducing MEZO Gauges](https://mezo.org/blog/introducing-mezo-gauges)), and DIA lists MUSD below $1 on an undated reading, with protocol-controlled value holding about 15.0M MUSD of the bootstrap loan on 2026-10-02 ([DIA](https://www.diadata.org/map/global-stablecoins/stablecoin/musd-mezo/)). On size, MUSD supply grew from about 18.9M in March to about 30M by mid-September 2026 ([Yellow](https://yellow.com/asset/mezo-usd), [Yearn curation](https://curation.yearn.fi/report/mezo-musd/)), which is modest for a chain whose veBTC alone holds $64.56M. One DefiLlama-based analysis has Mezo's total TVL falling from a $76.3M peak to about $23.3M by April 2026 ([Yearn curation](https://curation.yearn.fi/report/mezo-musd/)), though veBTC climbed back to its high in October. On security, an attacker took about **5.52 BTC from 37 veBTC positions** on 2026-10-05. Mezo paused the chain and bridge, fixed the bug the next day, and now plans to **shut down native BTC wallet sign-up and bridge-ins** and tighten bridge-out limits ([Incident report](https://mezo.org/blog/incident-report-october-5-2026)). Earlier in the year, Velar's PerpDEX on Mezo was exploited ([Mezo blog](https://mezo.org/blog/velars-perpdex-exploited-on-mezo)).

Put together, Mezo pays for four things: MUSD that gets minted and held, MEZO that gets locked, BTC that arrives through doors other than the native bridge, and careful, audited code. A single Feasy deposit hits all four. It mints MUSD in a trove, holds it as sMUSD, locks BTC as veBTC and adds to a permanent veMEZO lock. That is Feasy's pitch to Mezo in one sentence.

Mezo's partners are things Feasy can plug into, not sources of money. **Mezo Prime** runs institutional BTC through Anchorage Digital enclaves at a 1% fixed rate, with Bullish as a 250 BTC first customer ([Mezo Prime](https://mezo.org/blog/introducing-mezo-prime/), [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/mezo-launches-institutional-bitcoin-yield-vaults-with-anchorage-digital-and-bullish-including-250-btc-anchor-investment/)). **Banxa** sells MUSD for fiat ([Banxa](https://banxa.com/press/mezo-integrates-with-banxa-to-enable-global-fiat-on-ramp-for-musd-and-bitcoin-backed-finance/)), **Wormhole** carries MUSD between chains with $750M of lifetime volume ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/musd-750m-volume-wormhole-network/)), and **Threshold** supplies tBTC ([Threshold](https://www.threshold.network/blog/threshold-x-mezo)). **Morpho** hosts an MUSD vault on Ethereum ([Morpho vault post](https://mezo.org/blog/stack-sats-and-bitcoin-backed-stables-morpho-vault-incentives-go-live)), and **Saturn's sUSDat**, backed by STRC, pairs with MUSD in a pool that can get a gauge ([STRC yield](https://mezo.org/blog/how-to-earn-strc-yield-on-mezo)).

Feasy isn't alone in this lane. Hackathon 2's winners include **Cermin**, which automates BTC-backed MUSD borrowing with a savings split and repays debt when BTC falls, **BynD**, which pools veMEZO and sends the boost to the best veBTC, **Fractals**, which splits veBTC and veMEZO into fractions, **Mezoir**, an intent agent for the ve-economy, and two savings products, **Cark** and **MezoPay** ([Hackathon 2 winners](https://mezo.org/blog/announcing-mezo-hackathon-2-winners/)). Beyond the hackathon, Boar Finance auto-votes delegated veBTC for a 10% fee ([Boar Finance](https://mezo.org/blog/set-it-and-earn-automated-vebtc-yield-with-boar-finance/)), Matchbox runs a community boost market ([Matching market](https://mezo.org/docs/users/mezo-earn/lock/vemezo/matching-market)), and Ditto lists an MUSD savings vault ([Mezo docs index](https://mezo.org/docs/llms.txt)). The research found no product that bundles a trove, sMUSD, the gauge, veBTC and a veMEZO boost behind one BTC deposit. The search was shallow, though, and nobody checked which hackathon projects are live on mainnet. The bigger threat is Mezo itself, since simpler Earn onboarding and a native veNFT marketplace are both on the roadmap.

## Every ve-chain grew a liquid locker, and Mezo's spot is nearly empty

Every vote-escrow economy so far has grown a liquid locker that took a big share of the locked supply and then steered emissions. **Convex** held **44% of veCRV** by early 2022 ([GSR](https://www.gsr.io/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/2022.1.21_Curve-Wars.pdf)) and peaked above $20B TVL ([FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/cryptocurrencies/news/defi-protocol-convex-finance-crosses-20b-in-locked-value-202201031038)). **Aura** needed Balancer to allowlist it in veBAL and asked Balancer for about $300k to cover audits ([Balancer forum](https://forum.balancer.fi/t/proposal-allowlist-aura-finance-in-balancer-votingescrow/2708?page=3), [Aura blog](https://blog.aura.finance/more-about-aura-finance-and-our-launch-plans)), then held over a quarter of veBAL by mid-2022. **Velodrome** got a 3M OP grant from the Optimism Foundation ([Optimism forum](https://gov.optimism.io/t/ready-gf-phase-1-proposal-velodrome-finance/3736)) and later built its own locker, Relay, which holds over 10% of veVELO by the team's count ([Velodrome Relay](https://medium.com/@VelodromeFi/velodrome-relay-d7cf5ff191f1)). **Penpie and Equilibria** launched in June 2023 and held about 24% and 26% of vePENDLE ([Nansen](https://research.nansen.ai/articles/the-pendle-wars-an-analysis-of-de-fi-s-latest-battleground)), and Penpie's parent, Magpie, asked Arbitrum for 1.25M ARB ([Arbitrum forum](https://forum.arbitrum.foundation/t/magpie-penpie-radpie-campie-final-stip-round-1/16648)).

The lesson for Feasy's ask is that the host rarely gave cash. It gave permission and audit money, and the lockers then paid their own way from emissions. The warning is that the yields fade. Convex's bribe yield peaked near 46% APR, then fell to about $1 of bribes for every $1.90 of emissions ([Delphi](https://members.delphidigital.io/reports/is-the-convex-flywheel-sustainable)). Convex now holds about $505M ([DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/protocol/convex-finance)).

On Mezo, that spot is nearly empty. Boar Finance, a third-party vault that votes deposited or delegated veBTC every week for a 10% performance fee, gathered only **10.6 BTC over its first five epochs** and has **no liquid receipt token**. Mezo still promoted it on its blog, while saying it wasn't endorsed ([Boar Finance](https://mezo.org/blog/set-it-and-earn-automated-vebtc-yield-with-boar-finance/)). A tiny incumbent and a host willing to market outside ve-products: that's the opening. In March, Mezo itself wrote that there were "no automated strategies yet for veMEZO" ([Aerodrome x MEZO](https://mezo.org/blog/aerodrome-x-mezo-2-25-of-token-supply-rewards)).

Mezo's veBTC design also changes the locker playbook in Feasy's favour. Locks last at most 28 days ([Mezo Earn overview](https://mezo.org/docs/users/mezo-earn/overview)), so a liquid veBTC token can always be redeemed within four weeks. It never has to hold a permanent market peg the way cvxCRV did. The docs read don't say whether veBTC and veMEZO NFTs can be transferred, but the roadmap's planned marketplace implies they can.

Other programs show what a sensible ask looks like. Stablecoin issuers pay for demand. Aave's Merit program spent **$5M over 90 days** in GHO and WETH ([Aave governance](https://governance.aave.com/t/arfc-merit-a-new-aave-alignment-user-reward-system/16646)). Curve DAO approved up to **$60M of crvUSD** for Yield Basis's BTC pools, in return for 7.5% of its token supply ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/curve-dao-approves-60m-crvusd-yield-basis-bitcoin-pools)). Consumer savings products attract chain money too. PoolTogether's Optimism grants of 450k and then 550k OP came before it reached $40M of deposits and 35,000+ depositors ([Optimism forum](https://gov.optimism.io/t/pooltogether-grant-2-update-moving-forward/5724)). Bitcoin DeFi chains fund vaults modestly. Stacks paid **1 BTC a month for 90 days**, split between Zest and Bitflow ([Stacks](https://www.stacks.co/blog/stacks-90-day-defi-incentive-program-announced-with-zest-and-bitflow)), and per a search summary, BOB ran a 750k OP deposit program ([Blockmedia](https://www.blockmedia.co.kr/archives/864297)). Next to Mezo's $7,000 Buildathon and $3M accelerator, a Feasy ask belongs in the **tens to low hundreds of thousands of dollars in MEZO or veMEZO**, paid out in milestones. Anything bigger needs a number Mezo already watches.

### 1. Liquid veBTC with a pooled veMEZO booster, the bold one

**What Feasy builds.** Feasy already has the parts. It runs a 4-week ladder of veBTC locks, each with its own boost gauge, and a permanent veMEZO lock that votes boosts for them through BoostVoter. The step is to open both to outside users. **BTC holders** deposit into the ladder and get a liquid token that redeems at the next lock to mature, so nobody waits more than four weeks. **MEZO holders** lock into Feasy's permanent veMEZO and get a liquid MEZO receipt that earns a cut of the boosted rewards and the boost-gauge incentives, the same pattern as cvxCRV. **Feasy votes** the pooled veBTC toward the sMUSD gauge its own vault stakes in and toward MUSD pools, and sells spare boost through the matching market. **Part of the 10% performance fee** buys MEZO for the permanent lock, the treasury step Feasy already plans for after Wave 2.

The end state is the bold part. Feasy would control the largest steerable block of veBTC votes and veMEZO boost on Mezo, the layer Mezo's roadmap describes and no one has built.

**Why Mezo funds it.** It turns three roadmap items into one product: a veNFT marketplace for veBTC and veMEZO, voting delegation and simpler Earn onboarding ([Mezo roadmap](https://mezo.org/blog/mezo-2026-roadmap)). Every permanent lock takes MEZO off the market while the token sits about 90% below its high and veMEZO is worth about 2.6% of veBTC. The precedents are strong: Convex took 44% of veCRV, Aura over a quarter of veBAL, Penpie and Equilibria about half of vePENDLE between them, and Velodrome's own Relay over a tenth of veVELO.

**Funding route.** Ask Ignition for a milestone grant **paid in veMEZO straight into the booster**, the same form Mezo used for its Encode prizes. Tie the milestones to BTC locked as veBTC through Feasy and MEZO locked permanently. In the same conversation, ask for the integration "yes" that Aura needed from Balancer: confirmation that veNFTs can be transferred and that a pooled booster is welcome. Meanwhile, collect Academy veMEZO, and once the Grant Committee exists, bring it the proven numbers.

**Main risks.** The first is that Mezo builds it first: its own marketplace and delegation could make the wrapper redundant, so Feasy has to ship before they do. The second is governance power. Only veBTC holders vote in Mezo governance ([Governance](https://mezo.org/docs/users/mezo/governance/)), so a large locker gains political power that Mezo may resist; the counterargument is the 28-day lock, because depositors can leave within a month and the block can't entrench itself. The third is security. veBTC was the target of the October 5 exploit ([Incident report](https://mezo.org/blog/incident-report-october-5-2026)), so this idea faces the highest audit bar of the five. Boar Finance, BynD and Fractals already work this lane, Convex's history shows bribe yields fading, and at today's MEZO price the rewards are small in dollars, so the pitch has to rest on BTC yield and lock-in, not on emissions.

### 2. fBTC: Feasy's vault share as a pool pair and collateral

**What Feasy builds.** Make the vault share a token people trade and borrow against. Get fBTC whitelisted on PoolsVoter, open an fBTC/MUSD pool on Mezo Pools, call `createPoolGauge`, and point Feasy's own veBTC votes at it. Then list an fBTC/MUSD market on Aerodrome and next to the tBTC/MUSD and cbBTC/MUSD markets in the Morpho alpha-musd vault on Ethereum ([Morpho vault post](https://mezo.org/blog/stack-sats-and-bitcoin-backed-stables-morpho-vault-incentives-go-live)).

**Why Mezo funds it.** Liquidity is the problem Mezo writes about most: "MUSD is only useful if it's liquid where people already are" ([Introducing MEZO Gauges](https://mezo.org/blog/introducing-mezo-gauges)). Every fBTC/MUSD pool adds MUSD depth that pays its own yield. The roadmap asks for BTC yield vaults across risk profiles. Curve DAO's credit line to Yield Basis shows that a stablecoin issuer will back a BTC product that grows its stablecoin, in return for a share of that product ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/curve-dao-approves-60m-crvusd-yield-basis-bitcoin-pools)).

**Funding route.** Ask Mezo to whitelist fBTC for the pool gauge, and ask governance to allowlist an off-Mezo fBTC pool in MEZO Gauges. Apply to the Founder Program once fBTC has holders. A Yield Basis-style MUSD credit line is a stretch ask with no Mezo precedent.

**Main risks.** fBTC already holds a trove loan inside it. Borrowing MUSD against fBTC stacks a second loan on the same BTC, so a sharp drop liquidates both layers. A share-price oracle can be manipulated. Mezo documents no whitelist process, and early pools will be thin.

### 3. Wrapped-BTC intake from Ethereum and Base

**What Feasy builds.** One-click deposits of tBTC, WBTC or cbBTC from Ethereum and Base that land in the vault. MUSD can go back out to other chains through Wormhole, Mezo Passport covers an Alpha Builder requirement, and Banxa offers a fiat start. Feasy's app already ships the tBTC-from-Ethereum route, which has been the only retail way in since Bitcoin bridge-in closed in mid-October.

**Why Mezo funds it.** The roadmap's first pillar wants Mezo to be the home of wrapped BTC earning yield ([Mezo roadmap](https://mezo.org/blog/mezo-2026-roadmap)). Mezo plans to shut down native bridge-ins after the exploit ([Incident report](https://mezo.org/blog/incident-report-october-5-2026)), which makes every other way in more valuable. Mezo has paid to bring BTC in before, with up to 2.5% of supply for "Bring Bitcoin Home" according to a secondhand report ([finanzen.net](https://www.finanzen.net/nachricht/aktien/mezo-opens-pre-deposit-vaults-to-migrate-bitcoin-liquidity-from-ethereum-back-to-bitcoin-native-infrastructure-15410512)). It also fits the Buildathon's Access & Distribution track directly.

**Funding route.** The Access & Distribution track if Mezo opens another round, Ignition, and a migration campaign marketed jointly with Mezo.

**Main risks.** Bridges are where Mezo has been hurt. This research didn't check which wrapped assets Mezo accepts or how they become trove collateral. Mezo's own simpler Earn onboarding could cover the same path, and the idea is less striking than the first two.

### 4. No-loss prize savings on sMUSD

**What Feasy builds.** A prize sleeve. Savers keep their principal, and each week the sMUSD yield and MEZO emissions go to a few winners, with Mezo topping up prizes in MEZO.

**Why Mezo funds it.** PoolTogether's two Optimism grants came before it reached $40M of deposits ([Optimism forum](https://gov.optimism.io/t/pooltogether-grant-2-update-moving-forward/5724)). Payments and commerce made up **45% of Hackathon 2's 64 submissions** ([Hackathon 2 winners](https://mezo.org/blog/announcing-mezo-hackathon-2-winners/)), so Mezo's builders and judges lean toward consumer products. Prizes paid in MEZO also cost Mezo little in dollars.

**Funding route.** The Founder Program, with a MEZO prize budget.

**Main risks.** Lottery and gambling law, and where the randomness on Mezo would come from; this research covered neither. PoolTogether's second grant cut the OP paid to depositors by 30%, which shows how much the appeal leans on subsidies. Mezo's docs list sMUSD at about 5% variable APR ([MUSD Savings Vault](https://mezo.org/docs/users/mezo-earn/vaults/musd-savings-vault)), which makes for thin prizes without top-ups. Cark and MezoPay overlap.

### 5. Institutional share class through Mezo Prime

**What Feasy builds.** A permissioned share class that takes the MUSD Mezo Prime clients borrow and runs it through sMUSD, the gauge and the booster, with reports for custodians.

**Why Mezo funds it.** Institutional access is a roadmap pillar ([Mezo roadmap](https://mezo.org/blog/mezo-2026-roadmap)). Prime launched with Anchorage, 1% fixed borrowing and Bullish's 250 BTC ([Mezo Prime](https://mezo.org/blog/introducing-mezo-prime/)).

**Funding route.** Business development with Mezo and Anchorage, plus the accelerator for runway.

**Main risks.** Mezo already runs both sMUSD's strategy and Prime itself. Compliance costs are high, and institutional sales take a long time for a small team.

## Conclusion

The research changes what Feasy should ask for. Mezo isn't short of dollars to hand out so much as short of demand for MEZO and BTC that stays, and its cheapest currency is its own token. A veMEZO milestone grant big enough to make Feasy a top booster costs Mezo little in cash and comes back to it as locked supply. So Feasy should ask for veMEZO, permission (whitelisting, transferability, featuring) and audit money, not cash. And it should pitch each deposit as four Mezo numbers moving at once: MUSD minted, sMUSD held, BTC locked as veBTC and MEZO locked for good.

Plan the next phase around Ignition rather than a Wave 3 that no source announces. The top three ideas also fit together into one path: wrapped BTC from any chain comes in, fBTC and liquid locker tokens come out, and the pooled votes steer MUSD liquidity where Mezo wants it. Mezo's own roadmap sets the deadline. Once its veNFT marketplace and delegation ship, a locker stops being a product and becomes a feature, so the locker and its audit come first.
